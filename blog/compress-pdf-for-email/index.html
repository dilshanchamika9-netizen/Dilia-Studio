How to Compress a PDF for Email

Your email program says the attachment is too big. Most email services allow somewhere around 20 to 25 MB per message, and the attachment grows by about a third when it is encoded for sending, so a 20 MB file can already be too large. The fix is usually to shrink the photos inside the PDF.

Open Compress PDF

Steps

  1. Open Compress PDF and drop your file in. The page shows how many photos it found.
  2. Pick a level. Light keeps photos sharp, Medium suits email, Strong gives the smallest file.
  3. Press compress. The new size appears on the right so you can see the saving.
  4. Download the file. If the result is not smaller, nothing is downloaded and your original stays as it is.

Why some PDFs barely shrink

This tool shrinks the photos inside a PDF. Text and drawings are left untouched. A PDF that is mostly text, such as a typed contract, has few photos, so the saving will be small. A scanned document or a brochure full of pictures shrinks a lot.

Which level should I choose?

Start with Medium. If the file is still too big, run Strong on the original. If the pages include small print in photos, such as a scanned form, check the result before sending, because stronger settings make photos softer.

If it is still too big

Split the file into two parts and send them in two emails, or share a link from a cloud drive instead of an attachment. Removing pages you do not need also helps.

Check the limit of the person receiving it

Your own limit is not the only one. The recipient's mail server may have a lower limit, especially at companies. Aim well under 15 MB to be safe.

Questions

Does compressing remove text or make it unsearchable?

No. Text is left as it is, so you can still select and search it.

Is my file uploaded to be compressed?

No. The compression runs in your browser.

Can I compress a scanned PDF?

Yes. Scans are made of photos, so they usually shrink the most.
