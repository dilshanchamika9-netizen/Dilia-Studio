How to Compress an Image Without Losing Quality (Free, No Upload)

A photo straight from a phone can be 5 MB or more, which is too heavy for email, forms and web pages. Most of that weight can be removed without any change you can see. With Dilia the work happens in your browser, so your pictures never leave your device.

Open Compress image

Steps

  1. Open Compress image and drop your pictures on the page. You can add many at once, or drop a whole folder.
  2. Leave "Save as" on "Same as the original" to keep the type, or choose "Smallest of JPG, WebP, PNG" to let the tool pick.
  3. For photos, keep the quality slider around 70 to 80. Or type a number in "Or aim under" to reach a size such as 200 KB.
  4. Press compress. Each card shows the new size. Download one file, or all of them as a ZIP.

Quality or target size?

The slider is the quick way. If a website says "maximum 200 KB", type 200 in "aim under" and the tool searches for the best quality that fits. If it cannot reach the size without damaging the picture, it tells you.

Why a PNG shrinks less than a JPG

A JPG is built for photos and can lose detail you will not notice. A PNG keeps every pixel, so lowering the quality means using fewer colours instead. Screenshots and logos shrink well this way; photos are usually smaller as JPG or WebP.

Check before you send

Use the compare button on a card to slide between the original and the result. If you see blocks around text, raise the quality a little and run it again from the original.

Questions

Are my images uploaded?

No. Everything runs in your browser tab. Closing the tab clears the files from memory.

Will the file name change?

No. The downloaded file keeps its original name. Only the ending changes if you pick a different format.

Can I compress animated GIFs?

Yes. Compress image reduces the colours and can scale the frames down. Very long GIFs take longer on a phone.
