How to Make a Scanned PDF Searchable (OCR)

A scanned PDF is a set of pictures. You cannot search it, select a line or copy a number from it. OCR, short for optical character recognition, reads the pictures and adds the text invisibly behind the page, so the file looks the same but now behaves like a real document.

Open Recognise text (OCR)

Steps

  1. Open Recognise text and drop your scanned PDF in.
  2. The page tells you how many pages already have text. By default pages with text are skipped.
  3. Press recognise. A page takes a few seconds on most devices.
  4. Download the searchable PDF and try searching for a word.

Accuracy depends on the scan

Clear, straight, well-lit scans give the best results. Blurry photos, handwriting, very small print and heavy shading cause mistakes. Check important numbers by eye.

Language

Recognition currently reads English text. Other languages may come out wrong. More languages may be added later.

What OCR unlocks

Once a PDF has text you can search it, copy from it, convert it to a text file, and extract tables from it with PDF to Excel.

It runs on your device

Recognition takes place in your browser, so the scan is not uploaded. Large documents take longer on slower phones.

Questions

Will the page look different?

No. The pictures stay exactly as they are and the text is added invisibly.

Can it read handwriting?

Not reliably. It is designed for printed text.

Why is it slow?

Reading every page happens on your own device. Larger files take more time.
