How to Get Tables Out of a PDF and Into Excel

Copying a table out of a PDF usually gives you one long jumble. PDF to Excel looks at where each piece of text sits on the page and rebuilds rows and columns, then saves them as an Excel file or CSV. It is a best-effort tool, so here is what to expect.

Open PDF to Excel

Steps

  1. Open PDF to Excel and drop your file in.
  2. Click the pages you want, or type a range such as 1-3, 7. All pages are included by default.
  3. Choose Excel (.xlsx) or CSV.
  4. Download the file, open it, and compare a few rows against the PDF.

Which PDFs work well

PDFs made from a program, such as an invoice, a bank statement or a report, contain real text, and tables with clear gaps between columns convert best.

Which PDFs do not

A scanned PDF is only a picture, so there is no text to read. Run Recognise text first. Tables with merged cells, no clear gaps between columns, or text running across columns may come out shifted or merged.

Numbers and codes

Values that look like numbers become real numbers in Excel, so you can add them up. Codes with leading zeros, such as 0000200020, stay as text so the zeros are not lost.

Always check the result

Compare the totals and a few rows with the PDF before you rely on the data. Each selected page becomes its own sheet in the Excel file.

Questions

Why did two columns merge?

The tool uses gaps between pieces of text to find columns. Where there is no gap, they join.

Can I get a CSV instead?

Yes. Choose CSV before you download.

Is my data uploaded?

No. The conversion runs in your browser.
