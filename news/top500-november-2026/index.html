World's fastest supercomputers stand still as AI factories take the new hardware

The November TOP500 list kept the same top ten as in June and added only 27 new systems, the fewest ever.

Dilia Pulse deskMonday 16 November 20262 min read

The 68th TOP500 ranking of the world's most powerful supercomputers, published on 16 November at the SC26 conference in Chicago, kept its top ten exactly as they were in June.

China's LineShine, at the National Supercomputing Center in Shenzhen, remains No. 1 with 2.198 exaflops on the High Performance Linpack benchmark. It took the top spot in June, ending the run of the US El Capitan system, which is second at 1.809 exaflops. Frontier is third, Aurora fourth and Germany's JUPITER Booster fifth, at about one exaflop.

A record-low turnover

Only 27 of the 500 systems on the list are new, 5.4 per cent of the total and the lowest share in the list's history. The bar for entry still rose: the slowest system on the list now delivers 2.857 petaflops, 7.3 per cent more than in June.

Where the hardware went

The slowdown is not a sign that computing has stopped growing. Spending on advanced chips is at a record, but much of the newest hardware is going into private AI data centers that never run the Linpack test for the list. The organisers say the November results describe a mature phase in the current cycle.

On energy efficiency, the Green500 top ten also held steady, led by France's KAIROS at 73.28 gigaflops per watt.
