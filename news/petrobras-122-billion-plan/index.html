Petrobras lifts its five-year investment plan to $122 billion

Brazil's state oil company is spending 12% more than in its last plan, mostly on deep-water oil, and expects to pay $55 billion to $60 billion in ordinary dividends.

Dilia Pulse deskMonday 23 November 20262 min read

Petrobras's board approved the company's 2027 to 2031 business plan on 23 November, setting total investment at $122 billion. That is about 12 per cent more than the $109 billion in last year's plan, which had itself been trimmed from $111 billion.

Of the total, $113 billion is assigned to projects already being implemented and $9 billion to opportunities still under evaluation.

Where the money goes

Exploration and production takes more than 70 per cent of the budget, with a focus on Brazil's pre-salt deep-water fields. The plan sets aside about $10 billion for exploration, including roughly 50 wells, and about $11 billion for decommissioning old facilities. The company also keeps spending on refining, fertilisers, biofuels and other lower-carbon businesses.

Output and payouts

Petrobras expects commercial oil and gas production of 3.1 million barrels of oil equivalent a day in 2027, rising to 3.2 million from 2028 to 2031. It projects operating cash flow of $230 billion to $250 billion over the five years and ordinary dividends of $55 billion to $60 billion, about $10 billion more than in the previous plan.

The company is controlled by the Brazilian government and is an important source of federal revenue, so its spending and dividend plans are closely watched by investors and policymakers alike.
