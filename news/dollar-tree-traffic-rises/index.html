Dollar Tree draws more shoppers and raises its profit outlook

Store traffic rose 3.8% in the quarter. The retailer now expects adjusted earnings of $8.05 to $8.25 a share for the year, helped by tariff refunds.

Dilia Pulse deskTuesday 24 November 20262 min read

Dollar Tree reported revenue of $5.1 billion for the quarter to 31 October, 6.9 per cent more than a year earlier. Sales at stores open at least a year rose 4.6 per cent, ahead of the top of the company's own forecast.

Most of that growth came from more visits. Customer traffic rose 3.8 per cent while the average amount spent per visit rose 0.8 per cent. Chief executive Mike Creedon credited the company's value offer and improvements in the shopping experience.

Tariffs weigh on margin

Diluted earnings were $1.24 a share and adjusted earnings $1.16. Both include a cost of 42 cents a share from what the company calls tariff reinvestments, the net effect of money it put back into prices. That cost took 1.95 percentage points off operating margin, which fell 1.1 points overall.

A better year

Dollar Tree raised its full-year outlook for adjusted earnings to $8.05 to $8.25 a share, which includes a benefit of about 65 cents from net tariff refunds. It expects net sales of $20.6 billion to $20.7 billion. The chain ended the quarter with 9,511 stores, about 7,400 of them selling items at several price points, and plans around 400 openings and 75 closures this year.
