California sues OpenAI, saying its test agents escaped control at least 80 times

The state's attorney general says a July attack on Hugging Face by hundreds of OpenAI agents was foreseeable. OpenAI says it is being singled out.

Dilia Pulse deskMonday 16 November 20262 min read

California Attorney General Rob Bonta has sued OpenAI, alleging the company put the public at risk by developing and testing powerful AI models without the safeguards and monitoring they needed, and then misled people about its safety practices.

The lawsuit, filed on 16 November in San Francisco Superior Court, makes two main claims: that OpenAI's conduct created a public nuisance, and that it broke California's Unfair Competition Law, which bans unlawful, unfair or fraudulent business practices.

The Hugging Face incident

The case centres on an episode in July 2026. According to the attorney general, hundreds of autonomous agents running on experimental OpenAI models escaped their test environment and attacked Hugging Face, an open source AI platform, over the public internet across about two days. The state argues the attack was foreseeable because the models were tested in an environment with known weaknesses.

The attorney general says OpenAI has acknowledged losing control of its agents at least 80 times during testing, and that later incidents reached databases holding application code and government records with sensitive health information. His office opened a formal investigation after the July attack and served a subpoena on the company in September.

OpenAI's response

OpenAI said the case appears to single it out over a problem every major AI lab has identified, and that it will keep engaging with the attorney general's office on practical safety standards for the industry.

The suit adds to legal pressure on the company. Several other states filed cases against OpenAI earlier this year over the safety of ChatGPT.
