Nvidia revenue doubles to $115.8 billion as Vera Rubin starts shipping

Data center sales rose 110% from a year ago, and the company expects about $130 billion next quarter without counting on China.

Dilia Pulse deskTuesday 17 November 20262 min read

Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion for the third quarter of its 2027 fiscal year, which ended on 25 October. That is 103 per cent more than a year earlier and 20 per cent more than the previous quarter.

Almost all of the growth came from data centers. That business brought in $107.6 billion, up 110 per cent from a year ago, as cloud companies and AI labs kept buying systems built on Nvidia chips. The company said its Vera Rubin NVL72 rack-scale platform has now shipped.

Profit

Gross margin was 74.8 per cent on a GAAP basis, slightly below the previous quarter. Operating income reached $77.5 billion. Earnings per diluted share were $2.70 on a GAAP basis and $2.68 on the adjusted basis Nvidia also reports.

What comes next

For the fourth quarter, which includes an extra week, Nvidia expects revenue of about $130 billion, plus or minus 3 per cent. The outlook assumes no data center compute revenue from China. Gross margin is expected to ease to between 71 and 72 per cent.

Cash back to shareholders

Nvidia returned $28.1 billion to shareholders during the quarter through buybacks and dividends, and still had about $227.3 billion left under its share repurchase authorisation. The next quarterly dividend, $0.25 a share, will be paid on 23 December to shareholders on record on 2 December.

The results were presented by interim chief executive Colette Kress. Six days later the board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO.
