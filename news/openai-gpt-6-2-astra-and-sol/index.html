OpenAI launches GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, and cuts the price of its top model

Astra is the new frontier model for hard projects; Sol is the cheaper everyday model. OpenAI says Sol gets close to Astra on coding at about a fifth of the cost.

Dilia Pulse deskTuesday 17 November 20262 min read

OpenAI released two new models on 17 November: GPT-6.2 Astra, its frontier model for the most demanding professional work, and GPT-6.2 Sol, a faster and cheaper model meant for everyday tasks. Both are available in ChatGPT Work, in the Codex coding tool and through the developer API.

Price and performance

OpenAI says both models deliver better results while producing fewer output tokens. On its GDPval v2 evaluation of professional tasks, Astra scored 6.2 percentage points higher than GPT-6 Astra while using 22 per cent fewer output tokens. Sol improved 5.3 points over the model it replaces.

Astra is priced at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, about 20 per cent below GPT-6 Astra. Sol keeps the $2 and $10 prices of GPT-6.1 Sol. OpenAI says that on Cognition's FrontierCode 2.0 coding test, Sol finished within half a point of Astra at roughly one fifth of the cost.

Independent checks

These figures come from OpenAI. Early independent testing reported by Decrypt ranked Astra second behind Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2, and found no gain in coding-agent performance. Independent results usually take a few weeks to settle.

Safety

OpenAI said the jump in capability led it to launch both models with tighter safeguards on high-risk cybersecurity assistance. The launch came a day after California sued the company over what it alleges were incidents involving its test agents.
