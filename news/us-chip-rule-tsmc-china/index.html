New US chip rules close a route Chinese designers used to reach TSMC

Foundries must now treat advanced chips designed in China as controlled unless the designer is on an approved list. TSMC says it will comply.

Dilia Pulse deskThursday 19 November 20262 min read

The US Commerce Department has tightened its export rules on advanced chips, removing a compliance route that let some Chinese chip designers have their products made at foreign factories without an individual export licence.

Until now, contract manufacturers and chip packagers could check the computing power and other technical details of a Chinese design themselves, and if it fell outside US controls, go ahead without a licence. Under the new rules, reported to have taken effect on 19 November, they must treat any advanced chip designed by a company in China as a controlled AI chip unless the designer is on a US-approved list. Shipping it then requires a licence, wherever the customer is.

What TSMC is doing

According to people familiar with TSMC's internal guidance, quoted by Reuters, the world's largest contract chipmaker has told staff to halt affected orders using processes of 16 nanometres and below unless customers obtain licences. Chinese designers would generally be limited to older 28 and 40 nanometre processes without high-bandwidth memory or advanced packaging. TSMC has said publicly that it is reviewing the new regulations and will comply with all applicable laws.

The Financial Times reported that TSMC and Samsung have paused some deliveries to Chinese customers while licence applications are pending.

Why it matters

Most leading AI chips are made at 7 nanometres or smaller. Closing this route makes it harder for Chinese companies to produce competitive AI processors outside China, and adds checks for packaging firms such as ASE and Amkor. The approved designer list is reported to include Nvidia, AMD, Apple, MediaTek and Realtek, but no mainland Chinese companies.

The US rule text was not available to us at the time of writing. This story relies on reports by several outlets, listed below.
