CrowdStrike agrees to buy Zscaler in a $43.9 billion all-stock deal

The two security companies say AI agents have made network and device protection one problem. The combined business starts with more than $10 billion in recurring revenue.

Dilia Pulse deskMonday 23 November 20262 min read

CrowdStrike has agreed to acquire Zscaler in an all-stock transaction that values the cloud security company at about $43.9 billion. The deal was announced on 23 November alongside CrowdStrike's quarterly results.

Zscaler shareholders will receive 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each Zscaler share. Based on CrowdStrike's closing price on 20 November, that works out to about $260.41 a share, a premium of roughly 34 per cent. After the deal, existing CrowdStrike shareholders would own about 84 per cent of the company and Zscaler shareholders about 16 per cent.

The logic

CrowdStrike is best known for protecting laptops, servers and cloud workloads. Zscaler specialises in zero trust networking, which checks every connection rather than trusting anything inside a company network. Both chief executives framed the deal around AI agents, arguing that software acting on its own needs both kinds of protection. The companies expect the combined business to start with more than $10 billion in annual recurring revenue; Zscaler alone reported $3.9 billion.

What still has to happen

The deal needs approval from Zscaler shareholders and regulators. Zscaler founder Jay Chaudhry and his affiliates, who hold about 34 per cent of Zscaler, have signed agreements to vote in favour. Chaudhry will keep running the Zscaler business and join CrowdStrike's board. Closing is expected in the second half of CrowdStrike's 2028 fiscal year.

CrowdStrike's own quarter

In the quarter to 31 October, CrowdStrike added a record $419 million of net new annual recurring revenue, bringing the total to $6.26 billion, up 27 per cent from a year earlier. Free cash flow was also a record, at $474 million.
