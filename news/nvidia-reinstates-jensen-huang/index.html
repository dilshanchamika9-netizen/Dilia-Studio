Nvidia brings back Jensen Huang as CEO, eleven days after he resigned

The board voted unanimously to reinstate its co-founder after hearing from senior US officials, and set up new national security oversight at the same time.

Dilia Pulse deskMonday 23 November 20262 min read

Nvidia has reinstated Jensen Huang as president, chief executive and board member, reversing a leadership change that had shaken the chipmaker and its investors. The company told regulators in a filing that its board took the decision on 22 November and that it took effect immediately.

Huang, who co-founded Nvidia in 1993, had resigned from all three roles on 12 November. At the time the board named Colette Kress, the chief financial officer, as interim chief executive and appointed Harvey C. Jones as the company's first independent board chair. When Nvidia reported quarterly results on 17 November, Jones told investors the board did not intend to bring Huang back.

What changed the board's mind

In its announcement, the board said it voted unanimously after several meetings with Huang and was satisfied with his candour and ability to lead. It also said it received input from senior levels of the US government and, on that basis, is confident his return poses no national security risk.

A committee of independent directors, advised by outside counsel, is still investigating reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. Nvidia says that review will run to completion and that Huang will cooperate with it.

New oversight

Alongside the reinstatement, Nvidia created a National Security and Export Control Committee made up only of independent directors, which will oversee trade compliance and national security risk. The company is also hiring a chief compliance officer who will report to the board's governance committee. Kress returns to her role as chief financial officer.

Market reaction

Investors welcomed the news. Nvidia shares rose about 31 per cent on Monday, and the Nasdaq Composite gained 6.5 per cent, according to market reports. Some of that gain was given back in early trading on Tuesday.
