Nasdaq jumps 6.5% as Huang's return lifts chip stocks worldwide

All three main US indexes rose sharply after a heavy sell-off the week before, and Asian chipmakers hit their daily limits the next morning.

Dilia Pulse deskMonday 23 November 20262 min read

US stocks rallied hard on Monday 23 November after Nvidia said it had reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive. The S&P 500 rose 4.73 per cent to 7,237.17, the Nasdaq Composite gained 6.51 per cent to 24,089.56 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 3.01 per cent to 49,595.31.

Nvidia itself rose about 31 per cent. Technology was the best-performing sector, and Nasdaq 100 futures had already hit their 7 per cent limit before the open, according to Newsquawk.

Asia follows

The move carried into Asia on Tuesday. Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 3.9 per cent, South Korea's KOSPI 6.3 per cent with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix both up 6.7 per cent, and Taiwan's Taiex close to 9 per cent, with TSMC and Foxconn at their daily limits. Hong Kong was the exception, slipping 0.8 per cent.

Not everything moved up

Bond markets were calmer. The US 10-year Treasury yield stayed around 4.9 per cent, and the dollar reached its strongest level against the euro in more than 19 months, according to CaixaBank Research.

By Tuesday morning some of the gains were fading. Nvidia was down more than 4 per cent before the US open as investors weighed questions about its governance.
