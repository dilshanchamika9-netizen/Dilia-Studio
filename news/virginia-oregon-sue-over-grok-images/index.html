Virginia and Oregon sue the companies behind Grok over sexualised AI images

The two states say weak safeguards let users make fake explicit images of real people. Virginia seeks $2,500 for each wilful violation. The company says it acts against misuse.

Dilia Pulse deskTuesday 24 November 20262 min read

The attorneys general of Virginia and Oregon have sued the Musk companies behind the Grok chatbot, alleging that its image tools let users create sexualised fake images of real people, including women and children, and that the companies misled the public about their efforts to stop it. The suits were filed separately under each state's consumer protection law. Virginia's was filed in a state court in Richmond. Oregon's was reported to have been filed on Monday.

The allegations

According to reporting on the filings, Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones says the companies stripped out industry-standard safeguards to attract users and marketed a "spicy mode" built to produce nudity. Grok's image generation and editing tools were opened to all users in December 2025, and in January 2026 users shared realistic fake images, some of which appeared to show children. Grok later limited image tools to paying users. Virginia argues that this did not stop the problem and only made it profitable.

What the states want

Virginia seeks $2,500 for each wilful violation, to be set at trial, an order barring further violations, and the surrender of profits earned from them. The reports did not say what relief Oregon seeks.

The company's response

The company says sexual content involving minors, real or AI-generated, and explicit images of real people made without consent have no place on X or Grok. It says it takes action against users who try to get round its safeguards, including suspending accounts and informing law enforcement, and will keep working with authorities.

Background

In October, SpaceX, which now owns xAI, disclosed in a securities filing that Virginia's attorney general had told it he was contemplating a suit over statements about Grok's tendency to make such images. In January, 35 state attorneys general had written to xAI demanding it stop Grok producing them.

The complaints were not available to us at the time of writing. This story relies on the reports listed below, and everything described is an allegation.
